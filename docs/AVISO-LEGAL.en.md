## VPS inside or outside Brazil: what changes

The most common type of blocking in Brazil (e.g. the 2024 X/Twitter ban) is applied via court order to internet carriers (Vivo, Claro, TIM), not to datacenter/cloud providers. In practice, a VPS inside or outside Brazil tends to work the same for dodging that kind of block — your carrier only sees you talking to the VPS, never directly to the blocked service.

The real difference is jurisdiction: a VPS on Brazilian soil is more exposed to a future Brazilian court order (identifying the operator, demanding data, or even forcing the block at the source). A VPS outside the country requires international legal cooperation for that — slower and rarer.

If you feel this still leaves you exposed, or want extra resilience against future escalation, consider hosting outside Brazil. Some countries with a strong track record on free expression and privacy protections (reference: Freedom House's "Freedom on the Net", RSF's Press Freedom Index):

- **Iceland** — strong legal protection for press/speech freedom, little cooperation with foreign surveillance requests absent a local court order.
- **Switzerland** — strict privacy law, outside of Five/Nine/Fourteen Eyes surveillance-sharing agreements.
- **Netherlands or Germany** — within the EU, robust GDPR; part of Western intelligence-sharing networks, so good protection but not as isolated as Iceland/Switzerland.

This isn't legal advice — it's a starting point for research, not a substitute for consulting a lawyer if your use case is sensitive.
